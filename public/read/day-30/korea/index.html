Why did the founders name it **Gwangjang** (廣藏)? Originally, the market was planned on the reclaimed streambed between two historic stone bridges spanning Cheonggyecheon: **Gwanggyo** (광교, 'Broad Bridge') to the west and **Jangtonggyo** (장통교, 'Long Bridge') to the east—taking the first syllable of *Gwang* and *Jang*! When floods proved that narrow site unsuitable and the market was built slightly east on the old military garrison grounds of *Hullyeonwon* in Yeji-dong, the founders kept the name *Gwangjang* (廣藏), writing it with the characters meaning **'To Gather From Afar and Store in Abundance.'** Just as importantly, the secondary subsystems of Gwangjang Sijang (광장시장 Korea's first permanent covered market, 1905) were engineered to work in continuous feedback with the surrounding environment. 2. Food Science: Why Stone-Milled Mung Beans ('Nokdu') Create the Ultimate Crisp Crust

Why do Gwangjang Market vendors insist on grinding soaked hulled mung beans between wet granite millstones (maetdol) on-site rather than using dry commercial mung-bean flour?
