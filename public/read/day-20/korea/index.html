Key Context & Why It Matters

Formed between 100,000 and 10,000 years ago when parasitic volcanic cones (oreum) erupted rubbly a'a and blocky pahoehoe basalt lava flows across the island's flanks, Gotjawal terrains could never be cleared for farming. Beneath the mossy boulder piles lie deep subterranean air channels called sumgol ('breathing holes') that vent 15°C geothermal air year-round—allowing Siberian northern-limit ferns and tropical southern-limit camellias to grow on the exact same rock while filtering rainwater into Jeju's freshwater aquifers.
