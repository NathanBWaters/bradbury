Shin Saimdang's Chochungdo (1504–1551): The Botanist-Painter of Gangneung's Black-Bamboo House

Korea — Inside Korea's oldest residential wooden hanok, how a sixteenth-century woman painted watermelons, field mice, and katydids so lifelike that chickens pecked the silk (Mid-Joseon Dynasty (1504–1551)) • 20 min read

Key Context & Why It Matters

Born and raised inside Ojukheon ('Black-Bamboo House,' built in 1452 and preserved as Treasure No. 165 in Gangneung, Gangwon State), Saimdang defied the constraints of sixteenth-century patriarchal society to pioneer Chochungdo ('Grass and Insect Paintings')—rendering humble garden eggplants, watermelons, poppies, dung beetles, and field mice with an empirical entomological precision that anticipated European scientific illustration. Art-historical and scientific analyses of Shin Saimdang's Chochungdo screens (such as Treasure No. 595) highlight her mastery of combining fine-line contour drawing (gureukbeop) with 'boneless' translucent mineral pigment washes (molgolbeop, 몰골법).
