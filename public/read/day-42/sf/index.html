1. Material Science of Old-Growth Coast Redwood Balloon Framing

Why have Matthew Kavanaugh's 1892–1896 wooden row houses on Steiner Street survived both the magnitude 7.9 earthquake of 1906 and the magnitude 6.9 Loma Prieta quake of 1989 with minimal structural damage? 2. Butch Kardum and the 1963 Colorist Movement

Were Victorian houses originally painted in bright polychrome colors in the 1890s? Historic paint-chip microscopy shows that 1870s Stick and Italianate homes typically used muted earth tones (ochre, russet, olive, and sandstone brown), while 1890s Queen Anne houses began introducing livelier contrasts before twentieth-century white and battleship gray dulled the city. When artist Butch Kardum launched the San Francisco Colorist Movement in 1963—later codified by color consultants like Dr. Color (Bob Buckter) and chronicled by Pomada and Larsen in 1978—he fused 1960s psychedelic color theory with nineteenth-century woodcarving.
