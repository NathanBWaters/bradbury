Hanji (Chunji): Why Korea's Mulberry-Bark Paper Breathes for a Thousand Years

First, compare the raw botanical fiber. Western industrial paper is made by grinding whole softwood or hardwood tree trunks into short cellulose pulp (1 to 3 millimeters long) still loaded with **lignin**—the rigid phenolic polymer that stiffens tree wood—and bleaching it with chlorine and acidic aluminum sulfate (*alum* sizing). After simmering the white mulberry bark in *jaetmul* for four hours and bleaching it naturally in cold running mountain stream water and winter sunlight (*ilgwang-pyobaek*), the artisan refuses to chop the fibers with steel blades in a mechanical Hollander beater.
