Key Context & Why It Matters

2. Why the Reber Plan Failed: Evaporation Loss and the Tidal Prism

John Reber assumed that damming San Francisco Bay would conserve millions of acre-feet of freshwater. The Bay Model proved the exact opposite: because San Pablo Bay and the South Bay are broad and shallow, converting them into impounded lakes created a huge surface-area-to-volume ratio where summer winds evaporated more water than the dams saved. Worse, eliminating 85 percent of the bay's tidal prism crippled the natural ebb-tide jet that keeps the Golden Gate shipping channel scoured clear of ocean sandbars.
