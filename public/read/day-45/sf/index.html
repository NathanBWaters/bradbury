Key Context & Why It Matters

Rising **928 feet** above sea level in the geographic center of southwest San Francisco (37.7383° N, 122.4533° W)—twenty-four feet higher than Twin Peaks—stands **Mount Davidson**, the highest natural point in the City and County of San Francisco. Named in 1911 for pioneering U.S. Coast and Geodetic Survey astronomer and geographer **Professor George Davidson** (1825–1911), wrapped in a lush, moss-hung cloud forest of Tasmanian blue-gum eucalyptus planted in the 1880s by Comstock Lode tunnel engineer and Mayor **Adolph Sutro**, and saved from real-estate bulldozers in the 1920s by women's club activist **Madge Clairmont**, the summit is crowned by a **103-foot poured-concrete cross** lit via telegraph by President Franklin D. Roosevelt in 1934 and preserved in 1997 by San Francisco voters as the **Armenian Genocide Memorial**.

Standing today at the summit trail off Dalewood Way and Myra Way at 928 feet above sea level in the heart of Mount Davidson Manor, Miraloma Park, and Sherwood Forest (37.7383° N, 122.4533° W), a visitor who listens closely beneath the hum of Muni buses and the salt-laden Pacific breeze can still trace the physical contours of Sutro Forestry Era (1880s), Conservation Save (1926–1934) & 1997 Civic Resolution. Ask ten visitors or even long-time residents to name the highest natural peak in San Francisco, and nine will guess Twin Peaks (922 feet) or Mount Sutro (908 feet)—yet thirty-six blocks southwest of Market Street rises **Mount Davidson**, crowning the peninsula's central Franciscan chert ridge at **928 feet** above the tides of the Golden Gate. Every curbstone, cast-iron utility cover, and weathered timber along this corridor preserves the lived memory of how geodesist George Davidson (1825–1911), afforestation pioneer Adolph Sutro, preservationist Madge Clairmont, and the Council of Armenian American Organizations and a generation of San Franciscans confronted preserving San Francisco's highest peak as a wild fog-drip forest against 1920s subdivision developers and resolving a 1990s constitutional separation-of-church-and-state lawsuit through historic memory, transforming an improbably steep and seismically restless peninsula into an enduring laboratory of civic invention.

1. Microclimate Physics: How 'Occult Precipitation' Sustains a Summer Cloud Forest

Why is the soil inside Mount Davidson's forest damp and lush in mid-August when zero rain has fallen on San Francisco since May? Hydrologists call this phenomenon **fog-drip** or *occult precipitation*. Microscopic water droplets in summer marine advection fog are too light to fall as rain on bare grass, but when twenty-knot winds blow the fog bank through a 120-foot canopy of eucalyptus and cypress needles at 900 feet elevation, droplets coalesce on the leaf surfaces and patter to the ground—contributing up to ten inches of summer moisture that supports native ferns and coastal scrub. 2. Who Was Professor George Davidson (1825–1911)?

The man for whom San Francisco's highest peak was named in 1911 was the towering scientific figure of nineteenth-century California. Arriving in San Francisco in 1850 for the U.S. Coast Survey, George Davidson triangulated the exact latitude and longitude of every major promontory on the Pacific Coast, wrote the monumental *Coast Pilot of California, Oregon, and Washington* that guided thousands of ships safely through the fog, sited the Lick Observatory, and taught geography at UC Berkeley for decades.
